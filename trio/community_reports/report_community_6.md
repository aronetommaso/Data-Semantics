# Community Report: Levant and Arabian Peninsula (Community 6)

## Executive Summary

This report provides an analysis of conflict events within Community 6, encompassing the Levant and Arabian Peninsula, from 2016 to late 2023. The region is characterized by a complex interplay of state actions, internal dissent, and regional geopolitical shifts. Key conflict dynamics include the enduring Israeli-Palestinian conflict, systematic governmental suppression of opposition and religious freedoms in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia, and recurrent internal socio-economic and political unrest in Jordan. The period observed reveals a consistent pattern of state-led security measures, arrests, and suppression of civil liberties, often accompanied by localized violence and, more recently, widespread protests. The community's historical severity is notably high at 72.4, which has further escalated to a current severity of 78.3. This trajectory is compounded by an extremely high escalation risk of 95.9, indicating significant instability and potential for increased conflict intensity.

## Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict landscape within Community 6 is shaped by several prominent actors and their interactions:

*   **Government of Israel (GoI) and Palestinian Authorities (PNA, Hamas, PIJ):** The Israeli-Palestinian conflict remains a persistent source of instability. The GoI frequently implements extensive security measures, including closures of the West Bank and Gaza, checkpoints, settlement approvals, and responses to perceived threats. These actions often lead to retaliatory actions by Palestinian armed groups (Hamas, Palestinian Islamic Jihad) from Gaza, resulting in cycles of violence and subsequent Egyptian/UN-brokered ceasefires. The Palestinian National Authority (PNA) oscillates between security coordination with Israel and diplomatic efforts, while Hamas maintains de facto governance in the Gaza Strip.

*   **Government of Bahrain and its Security Forces:** Bahrain's government, primarily through its police forces and other security apparatus, engages in systematic suppression of the Shiite opposition. This includes mass arrests, politically motivated trials leading to lengthy sentences and citizenship revocation, dissolution of opposition political and religious societies (e.g., Al-Wefaq), and consistent interference with Shiite religious observances like Ashura (removal of banners, arrests). External actors, such as pro-government militias and US military support, also play a role in maintaining the state's power.

*   **Government of Saudi Arabia and its Security Forces:** The Saudi government frequently employs severe measures against dissent, notably mass executions on terrorism charges, which disproportionately affect the Shiite minority. Security forces conduct extensive operations, including demolitions of historic Shiite towns (e.g., Al Awamiyah in Al Qatif) under development pretexts, leading to forced evictions and property destruction. Arrests of alleged militants and critics, as well as broader state security measures, are common.

*   **Government of Jordan and Associated Civilian Groups:** Jordan experiences a mix of internal political and social challenges. The government responds to widespread public protests, often stemming from economic austerity measures, with temporary concessions (e.g., Prime Minister resignations, withdrawal of controversial bills). Labor unions and other civilian groups frequently organize strikes to voice demands. More recently, the country has witnessed instances of localized mob and tribal violence. The government also engages in diplomatic activities such as border re-openings (Syria) and land reclamation (Israel), while grappling with the societal impact of regional conflicts, particularly the Palestinian cause, which frequently triggers large-scale solidarity protests.

## Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The conflict events within Community 6 exhibit a discernible temporal evolution:

*   **2016-2017: Escalated Crackdowns and Security Measures:** This period saw significant state-led repression. Saudi Arabia conducted a large mass execution (47 fatalities in Jan 2016) and initiated property demolitions in Al Qatif. Bahrain dissolved major opposition groups (Al Wefaq, Waad), stripped citizenship from prominent clerics, and intensified arrests of activists and religious figures. Israel maintained a high tempo of security closures and movement restrictions in the West Bank and Gaza, often in response to attacks. Jordan faced student strikes and tightened border security following regional spillover.

*   **2018-2019: Persistent Repression and Cycles of Conflict:** Internal repression in Bahrain continued with mass trials, prolonged imprisonment of opposition leaders, and further suppression of religious expression. In Israel-Palestine, the period was marked by repeated cycles of rocket fire from Gaza and Israeli military responses, frequently mediated by Egypt and the UN to achieve temporary ceasefires. Jordan experienced widespread anti-austerity protests that led to government resignations and policy reversals. Saudi Arabia carried out another mass execution in April 2019 (37 fatalities, predominantly Shiite).

*   **2020-2023: Pandemic, Normalization, and Renewed Protests:** The COVID-19 pandemic introduced widespread lockdowns and public health-related security measures across all countries. Concurrently, the Abraham Accords saw Israel normalize relations with Bahrain and the UAE, a move met with internal dissent and protests in Bahrain. Repression in Bahrain persisted, including mass trials and continued interference with Ashura commemorations. Saudi Arabia continued its pattern of arrests and further executions. Jordan experienced internal tribal violence, various labor strikes, and, in late 2023, a significant surge in nationwide protests expressing solidarity with Palestinians in Gaza amid the latest round of violence, strongly condemning Israel and calling for the annulment of peace treaties. Israeli politics underwent significant instability with multiple elections and changes in government, alongside continued tensions and ceasefires with Gaza.

## Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The conflict dynamics within Community 6 have significant strategic and humanitarian repercussions:

*   **Strategic Impact:**
    *   **Internal Political Stability:** Governments in Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, and Jordan face ongoing challenges to their legitimacy due to a combination of internal dissent, human rights concerns, and economic pressures. The frequent cabinet reshuffles and policy reversals in Jordan highlight governmental vulnerability to public pressure.
    *   **Regional Alignment Shifts:** The Abraham Accords introduced new diplomatic alignments, notably between Israel and Bahrain/UAE, but simultaneously ignited strong popular opposition in countries like Bahrain and Jordan, posing dilemmas for governments attempting to balance regional integration with domestic sentiment.
    *   **Human Rights and Rule of Law Erosion:** The consistent patterns of mass arrests, politically motivated trials, executions, and suppression of basic freedoms (speech, assembly, religious practice) severely undermine human rights and the rule of law across several states in the community.
    *   **Economic Disruption:** Protests related to economic conditions (e.g., austerity, fuel prices in Jordan) and security closures (e.g., Gaza checkpoints, West Bank lockdowns) disrupt economic activity and livelihoods, contributing to broader instability.
    *   **External Influence:** The involvement of external actors (e.g., US military aid, alleged Iranian backing) underscores the region's geopolitical importance and the potential for outside forces to shape or exacerbate internal conflicts.

*   **Humanitarian Consequences:**
    *   **Fatalities and Injuries:** Direct fatalities, though varying in number, occur from state executions, attacks by armed groups, and inter-communal violence. Injuries result from various forms of confrontation and repression.
    *   **Detention and Imprisonment:** A significant humanitarian concern is the large number of political prisoners, often subjected to prolonged detention, mass trials, and allegations of torture, particularly in Bahrain.
    *   **Forced Displacement and Property Destruction:** Policies involving the demolition of homes and historical areas (e.g., Al Qatif, Palestine) lead to forced evictions and displacement of local populations, exacerbating vulnerability.
    *   **Restrictions on Movement and Livelihoods:** Security measures like border closures, checkpoints, and restrictions on fishing zones severely impact daily life, access to essential services, and economic opportunities for affected populations, notably in Palestine.
    *   **Suppression of Freedoms:** The curtailment of freedom of expression, assembly, and religious practice has profound psychological and social impacts, fostering a climate of fear and disempowerment among affected communities.
