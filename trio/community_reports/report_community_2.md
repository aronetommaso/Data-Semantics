# Community Report: Iraq and Syria (Levant)

## Executive Summary

This report provides a geopolitical analysis of conflict events within "community_2," primarily encompassing Iraq and Syria (the Levant) from 2016 to 2023. The region is characterized by sustained, extreme violence, predominantly stemming from the activities of the Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) and the multifaceted counter-insurgency operations conducted by Iraqi and Syrian government forces, the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF), and a broad International Coalition involving various allied militias. The conflict data reveals a highly volatile environment marked by severe humanitarian consequences and a persistent, evolving threat landscape. The pre-calculated metrics underscore the enduring intensity of the conflict, with both historical and current severity rated at 100.0, and an escalation risk assessed at 84.1.

## Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict within this community is driven by a complex interplay of state, non-state, and international actors:

*   **Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL):** As the principal non-state actor, ISIL is responsible for a significant majority of the high-fatality attacks. Their tactics range from mass executions of civilians and tribal groups, suicide bombings, and complex attacks targeting security forces, to extensive use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and scorched-earth tactics. Despite substantial territorial losses, ISIL has demonstrated a persistent capacity for insurgency, adapting to asymmetrical warfare by conducting ambushes, assassinations, and terrorizing civilian populations in rural and remote areas.

*   **Iraqi Security Forces (ISF) & Allied Militias:** This umbrella term covers various iterations of the Iraqi military (2014-2026), Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), Counter-Terrorism Service (CTS), and Iraqi Police forces. These actors have been central to direct combat operations, territorial recapture efforts, and ongoing counter-insurgency campaigns against ISIL. Their operations often involve large-scale armed clashes, air/drone strikes, and efforts to clear liberated areas of unexploded ordnance and ISIL infrastructure.

*   **Global Coalition Against Daesh (GCAD) & International Partners:** The International Coalition, including forces from the United States and other nations, has provided critical air support, intelligence, and training to local forces. Turkish and Russian military forces also feature, conducting airstrikes and engaging in various operations, reflecting broader geopolitical interests in the region. Their involvement has been pivotal in weakening ISIL's conventional military capabilities.

*   **Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) & Syrian Regime Forces:** In Syria, the SDF (including YPG) have played a crucial role in combating ISIL, particularly in the northern and eastern regions, often supported by the Global Coalition. Syrian regime forces and their allied militias (such as Hezbollah and various pro-Iran militias) have also engaged ISIL, especially in central and eastern Syria, frequently clashing with ISIL over strategic areas and supply routes.

The dynamics of the conflict are characterized by a protracted struggle against ISIL's insurgency. While large-scale conventional warfare has subsided since 2017, ISIL’s ability to conduct devastating attacks, coupled with the persistent threat of explosive remnants of war (ERW), ensures a highly unstable environment. The presence of numerous armed groups, including tribal militias and various foreign-backed forces, further complicates the security landscape, occasionally leading to inter-group tensions or collateral damage.

## Temporal Evolution (2016-2023)

The conflict events in "community_2" exhibit a discernible evolution:

*   **2016-2017: Peak Conventional Warfare and Atrocities:** This period is defined by intense, large-scale battles for major urban centers like Mosul and Fallujah in Iraq, and Raqqa in Syria. Events frequently involved "Armed clash" and "Government regains territory" types, often resulting in hundreds of fatalities. ISIL committed horrific atrocities, including mass executions of civilians (e.g., Mosul, Karadah bombings), while also suffering immense losses in coordinated offensives by Iraqi forces, PMF, Peshmerga, and the Global Coalition. Civilian casualties were exceptionally high, indicative of urban warfare and ISIL's brutal tactics.

*   **2018-2019: Insurgency and Consolidation:** Following the territorial defeat of ISIL, the nature of the conflict shifted towards a more fragmented insurgency. While large conventional clashes became less frequent, ISIL continued to conduct IED attacks, disrupted weapons use (often leading to accidental detonations), and targeted property destruction. The discovery of mass graves became a recurrent theme, underscoring the enduring legacy of ISIL's control. Iraqi and Syrian forces focused on search-and-destroy missions, defusal of explosives, and arresting ISIL operatives, reflecting a transition to counter-insurgency.

*   **2020-2023: Persistent Low-Level Conflict and Evolving Threats:** The most recent period shows a sustained, albeit lower-frequency, pattern of violence. ISIL remains active, particularly in remote areas, engaging in ambushes and IED attacks, notably targeting civilians (e.g., truffle pickers in Syria) and security forces. Explosive remnants of war continue to pose a significant threat to civilians across both Iraq and Syria. Government forces (Iraqi and Syrian) and the SDF maintain proactive security measures, including arrests, destruction of hideouts, and border deployments. While the scope of individual incidents may be smaller than the 2016-2017 peak, the persistent nature of ISIL's activities and the ongoing human cost highlight a deeply entrenched, long-term conflict. Recent events in late 2023, including ISIL attacks resulting in dozens of fatalities, demonstrate their continued capability for significant violence.

## Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The prolonged conflict in Iraq and Syria has had profound strategic and humanitarian repercussions:

*   **Strategic Impact:** The instability generated by ISIL and the ongoing counter-insurgency have severely hampered state-building efforts in both Iraq and Syria, diverting substantial resources from development to security. The region remains a flashpoint for international intervention and proxy conflicts, complicating regional stability. The persistent need for international military support highlights the enduring strategic challenge posed by extremist groups. The conflict has also triggered significant shifts in alliances and power balances across the broader Middle East.

*   **Humanitarian Consequences:** The human toll has been catastrophic. The data details mass executions, suicide bombings, and intense battles that have resulted in thousands of fatalities, including a disproportionate number of civilians. Widespread displacement, both internal and across borders, has created immense refugee crises. The destruction of infrastructure, livelihoods, and cultural heritage is immense. Furthermore, the prevalence of explosive remnants of war (ERW) continues to kill and maim civilians years after major fighting has ended, particularly in rural areas. The repeated discovery of mass graves serves as a grim reminder of the atrocities committed and the lasting trauma inflicted upon communities. The targeting of vulnerable populations, such as women (executed in Mosul) and truffle pickers, underscores the brutal and indiscriminate nature of the violence.
