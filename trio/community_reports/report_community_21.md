## Community Report: Shabwah Governorate Tribal Conflict Cluster, Yemen

### 1. Executive Summary

This report provides a geopolitical analysis of "community_21", characterized by inter-tribal conflict within the Shabwah Governorate of Yemen. The cluster is primarily defined by armed clashes between various tribal militias, predominantly centered on land disputes and other localized grievances. Key actors include the al-dayf-allah-tribal-militia-yemen, an-nisiyin-tribal-militia-yemen, and Al Maqarihah tribesmen. While historical severity has been moderate at 35.4, current severity has risen to 41.6, indicating a recent intensification of conflict. Critically, the escalation risk is assessed at a maximum of 100.0, highlighting an urgent and critical potential for increased violence and instability within the region.

### 2. Primary Actors & Dynamics

The primary actors within this cluster are tribal militias operating in Yemen's Shabwah Governorate. The most frequently observed active groups are:

*   **An Nisiyin Tribal Militia (an-nisiyin-tribal-militia-yemen):** This group is consistently involved in the majority of documented clashes, primarily with Al Maqarihah tribesmen. Their disputes often revolve around agricultural land ownership and, in at least one instance, the location of a communications tower.
*   **Al Dayf Allah Tribal Militia (al-dayf-allah-tribal-militia-yemen):** This militia has been involved in separate clashes, also with Al Maqarihah tribesmen, stemming from tribal disputes.
*   **Al Maqarihah Tribesmen:** Consistently appear as the opposing party in clashes with both An Nisiyin and Al Dayf Allah tribal militias, indicating their centrality to multiple conflict axes within the cluster.

The dynamics of conflict are predominantly characterized by armed clashes over localized resources, specifically land. Accusations of external influence, such as the Saudi-led coalition fueling tribal conflict, are noted by local sources. Furthermore, the withdrawal of IRG peacekeeping forces has been cited as a direct catalyst for the resumption of clashes, suggesting a delicate security environment highly susceptible to the absence of mediating or enforcing authorities.

### 3. Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The provided data covers events exclusively within 2023. Within this period, the conflict cluster demonstrates a fluctuating but intensifying trend:

*   **April 2023:** An initial isolated clash involving the Al Dayf Allah tribal militia resulted in fatalities.
*   **July 2023:** A brief resurgence of activity saw An Nisiyin factions clash internally, followed immediately by clashes between An Nisiyin and Al Maqarihah tribes over land ownership.
*   **September-October 2023:** This period marks a significant concentration and escalation of conflict. From mid-September into early October, An Nisiyin and Al Maqarihah tribes engaged in a sustained series of armed clashes in the Rammah, Zulaymin, and Markhah areas of Merkhah As Sufla district. These protracted engagements, largely over land and territory, included multiple days of consecutive violence and caused the closure of a vital road. The departure of IRG peacekeepers was explicitly linked to this resumption of hostilities.
*   **Late October 2023:** The period concludes with another isolated incident, involving Al Dayf Allah and Al Maqarihah tribes, resulting in a fatality, indicating persistent, albeit potentially sporadic, inter-tribal tensions.

Overall, while the year began with sporadic events, the cluster experienced a noticeable surge in sustained conflict activity during September and October 2023, predominantly between An Nisiyin and Al Maqarihah tribes.
