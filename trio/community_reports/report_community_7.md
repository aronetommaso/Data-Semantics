# Community Report: Northern Middle East and Arabian Peninsula Conflict Zones (Community 7)

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes the conflict dynamics within "community_7," a complex and highly volatile cluster encompassing regions in Turkey, Northern Iraq, Northern Syria, and the Saudi-Yemen border. The primary drivers of instability include the protracted conflict between the Turkish state and Kurdish armed groups (PKK/YPG), alongside severe incidents of violence targeting migrants at the Saudi-Yemen border involving the Saudi-led Coalition. The data spans from 2015 to 2023, revealing a consistent pattern of military operations, armed clashes, property destruction, and a deeply concerning rise in fatalities, particularly in the most recent years. The calculated metrics underscore an extremely high level of both historical and current severity, coupled with a substantial risk of further escalation.

## Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict events within this cluster are largely shaped by the interactions of several key actors:

*   **Turkish Security Forces:** This category encompasses the Military Forces of Turkey, Gendarmerie, and Police Forces. Their activities are predominantly focused on counter-insurgency operations against the PKK within Turkey, involving extensive arrests, weapons seizures, IED defusals, and destruction of militant infrastructure. Cross-border military operations, including airstrikes and ground movements, are frequently conducted in Northern Iraq and Syria against the PKK and Syrian Kurdish Forces (YPG/QSD). The 2016 coup attempt also saw significant clashes and fatalities involving Turkish military factions. Turkish forces are also heavily involved in intercepting and detaining migrants along their land and sea borders.

*   **Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) & Syrian Kurdish Forces (YPG/QSD):** These groups are the primary adversaries of the Turkish state. Their actions include armed clashes with Turkish forces in Turkey and Iraq, property destruction, IED attacks, and various forms of resistance and defensive measures in Syria. In Syria, the YPG/QSD (including the Syrian Democratic Forces - QSD, and the Menbij Military Council) have been involved in direct armed clashes with Turkish forces and Turkish-backed rebel groups, as well as undertaking security measures like digging tunnels and trenches, and redeploying forces in anticipation of Turkish offensives. They have also engaged in limited agreements with Russian forces.

*   **Saudi-led Coalition (Operation Restoring Hope):** Predominantly involving Saudi border guard forces, this actor is characterized by a disturbing pattern of shelling, artillery, and missile attacks targeting groups of migrants, predominantly Ethiopian, crossing the Saudi-Yemen border. These incidents, concentrated in 2022-2023, have resulted in extremely high fatalities. The coalition also has a history of detaining alleged spies and migrants in Yemen.

*   **Other Actors:**
    *   **Yemeni Factions:** Including Houthi-Saleh forces and the Hadi government, these actors are primarily involved in internal Yemeni dynamics, occasionally in the context of detentions or agreements related to foreign nationals.
    *   **Syrian Opposition Factions:** Groups such as the Syrian National Army (JWS) and National Liberation Front (JTW) act as allies or proxies of Turkey in Syria, engaging in clashes with YPG/QSD.
    *   **Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL):** Appears sporadically as a target of Turkish airstrikes or as an actor planting explosive devices.
    *   **Iranian and Greek Border Forces:** Involved in isolated incidents related to migrant movements, with the Greek forces being implicated in a mass migrant fatality event in 2022.

The overarching dynamics are defined by Turkey's sustained efforts to suppress Kurdish militancy within and beyond its borders, and the humanitarian crisis unfolding at the Saudi-Yemen border due to state-sponsored violence against migrants.

## Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The period under review demonstrates a fluctuating but consistently high level of conflict:

*   **2015:** Initial events primarily involve humanitarian aid deliveries and isolated incidents in Yemen, alongside early Turkish counter-insurgency activities in Turkey.
*   **2016:** Marked by significant escalation. Turkish counter-insurgency operations intensify in Turkey against the PKK, with numerous events of property destruction, arrests, and IED defusals. A major Turkish airstrike in Iraq results in 100 fatalities. The failed coup attempt in Turkey in July leads to substantial fatalities (84 in Ankara, 104 in Istanbul). PKK militants suffer fatalities from accidental detonation of their own explosives. Turkish forces begin arrests of migrants at borders.
*   **2017:** Turkish military involvement in Syria deepens, characterized by significant "Movement of Forces" and "Headquarters or base established" events in Idleb and Aleppo, and armed clashes with YPG in Afrin. Turkish airstrikes in Iraq continue (60 fatalities in January), followed by a large-scale strike in November (80 fatalities). PKK activity, primarily property destruction and IEDs, persists.
*   **2018:** Turkish cross-border operations in Syria continue, notably in Afrin (126 and 77 fatalities in March) and with large-scale troop mobilizations in Ras al-Ain and Tell Abiad. YPG/QSD actively prepare defenses, including digging tunnels and trenches. Turkish forces also record fatalities from accidental explosions during training.
*   **2019:** Turkish internal security operations against the PKK continue with substantial weapons seizures and destruction of shelters. Migrant arrests by Turkish forces on land and sea borders become a prominent event type. Joint Russian-Turkish patrols begin in Northern Syria.
*   **2020:** Turkish counter-PKK operations (e.g., "Yildirim" series) maintain a high tempo of weapon seizures and shelter destruction. A notable event includes PKK destroying a KDP headquarters in Al-Hasakeh. Turkish and Russian forces continue joint patrols in Syria.
*   **2021:** Turkish "Eren-Winter" ground operations against PKK intensify, marked by significant weapon/explosive seizures and destruction of shelters. PKK targets Turkish drones in Iraq. Migrant arrests remain frequent.
*   **2022:** A critical shift is observed with the emergence of extremely high-fatality events at the Saudi-Yemen border. Multiple events in November and December record 90, 150, and 150 migrant fatalities respectively due to Saudi-led Coalition shelling. A single incident at the Greek-Turkish border results in 19 migrant deaths. Turkish internal security operations and migrant arrests continue.
*   **2023:** The pattern of mass migrant fatalities at the Saudi-Yemen border continues unabated (e.g., 10, 185, 130, 40, 10 fatalities). Turkish airstrikes and shelling in Syria and Iraq against YPG/QSD and PKK targets intensify, causing significant fatalities (e.g., 6, 10, 10, 12, 10, 10, 40, 30 fatalities in individual events). PKK and Turkish forces engage in armed clashes in Turkey and Iraq. Iranian border police are implicated in a mass migrant killing (11 fatalities). This year highlights maximum intensity, particularly concerning migrant deaths and cross-border military engagements.

## Severity Assessment

The provided metrics indicate a severe and worsening conflict environment:

*   **Historical Severity (0-100): 92.5**
    This high historical severity score is justified by nearly a decade of persistent, multi-front conflict. The data consistently shows large-scale military operations, widespread property destruction, frequent arrests, and significant fatalities stemming from clashes between Turkish forces and Kurdish groups in Turkey, Iraq, and Syria. Notable high-casualty events, such as the 2016 coup attempt fatalities (188 total in Ankara/Istanbul in the provided data) and various Turkish airstrikes in Iraq/Syria, underscore a long-term pattern of intense violence and instability.

*   **Current Severity (0-100): 100.0**
    The maximum current severity score directly reflects the dramatic increase in high-fatality events in the most recent period (2022-2023). The numerous incidents of Saudi-led Coalition forces causing 90, 150, 150, and 185 migrant fatalities at the Saudi-Yemen border represent an unprecedented level of lethal violence. Concurrently, Turkish airstrikes and shelling in Syria and Iraq continue to result in substantial casualties among Kurdish fighters and civilians (e.g., 40 fatalities in one event, 30 in another in 2023). The sheer scale and frequency of these mass casualty events, combined with ongoing clashes and military operations, demonstrate that the conflict's intensity and human cost are at their highest level.

*   **Escalation Risk (0-100): 86.9**
    The substantial escalation risk is a direct consequence of the entrenched, multi-actor nature of the conflicts. The high current severity, characterized by lethal cross-border operations (Turkey into Syria/Iraq), persistent insurgent activities (PKK), and the extreme violence perpetrated against migrants at the Saudi-Yemen border, creates an inherently unstable environment. The involvement of multiple state and non-state actors, often with competing geopolitical interests and ongoing military buildups (e.g., Turkish troop movements in Syria), suggests a high likelihood of further intensification, geographical expansion, or the emergence of new, equally deadly conflict dynamics. The lack of clear resolutions to these core conflicts contributes to this elevated risk.

## Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The protracted conflicts within "community_7" carry profound strategic and humanitarian implications:

**Strategic Impact:**
*   **Regional Destabilization:** The conflicts are inherently cross-border, directly impacting the sovereignty and stability of Turkey, Iraq, and Syria. Turkey's extensive military presence and operations in northern Iraq and Syria challenge existing regional power balances and international norms.
*   **Complex Geopolitical Entanglements:** The involvement of external actors such as Russia (in joint patrols with Turkey in Syria) and the broader Saudi-led coalition in Yemen highlights the deep geopolitical complexities and proxy dynamics at play.
*   **Persistent Insurgency:** The enduring conflict between the Turkish state and the PKK/YPG ensures a continuous state of internal and cross-border security challenges, diverting significant resources and attention.
*   **Weaponization of Migration:** The extreme violence against migrants at the Saudi-Yemen border, alongside the use of migrants as political pawns or targets by various state actors, underscores a disturbing trend in regional border management and humanitarian responsibility.

**Humanitarian Consequences:**
*   **Mass Civilian Casualties:** The most severe humanitarian consequence is the high and escalating number of civilian fatalities, particularly from the indiscriminate violence against migrants at the Saudi-Yemen border. Civilians are also caught in the crossfire of Turkish operations in Syria and Iraq.
*   **Forced Displacement:** While not explicitly detailed in every event, the extensive military operations, shelling, and property destruction inevitably lead to large-scale internal and cross-border displacement, creating significant refugee and internally displaced person (IDP) crises.
*   **Destruction of Infrastructure:** The widespread property destruction, including homes, schools, hospitals (as seen in PKK actions) and critical infrastructure from airstrikes, severely impacts livelihoods and the ability of communities to recover.
*   **Human Rights Violations:** The reported tactics, especially the alleged Greek pushbacks resulting in fatalities and the documented mass killings of migrants at the Saudi-Yemen border, raise serious concerns regarding human rights, protection of civilians, and international humanitarian law.
*   **Impediments to Aid:** The volatile security environment, as evidenced by attacks on aid vehicles (e.g., CARE), complicates humanitarian access and the delivery of essential assistance to affected populations.
