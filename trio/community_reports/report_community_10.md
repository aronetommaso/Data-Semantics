# Community Report: Palestinian Refugee Camps (Community 10)

## 1. Executive Summary

Community 10, primarily encompassing Palestinian refugee camps in Lebanon (e.g., Ein el Hilweh, Mieh Mieh, Beddawi) and occasionally extending to Palestine, is characterized by complex inter-factional dynamics. Historically, the community experienced sporadic, localized incidents and attempts at de-escalation. However, the period leading up to 2023 witnessed a significant and alarming escalation in conflict activity, marked by frequent and lethal armed clashes. The current state is one of heightened tension and active confrontation, particularly within Ein el Hilweh, with a pronounced inability to sustain peace agreements. This trajectory indicates a severe and immediate risk of further escalation.

## 2. Primary Actors & Dynamics

The primary actor in this community is the **Fatah movement**, which is consistently involved in the majority of documented conflict events. Other significant actors include various Palestinian factions and militant groups, such as **Asbat al Ansar**, **Al Shabab al Muslim**, **Jund al Sham**, **Bilal Badr militia**, **Ansar Allah**, and **Hamas**.

The core dynamics revolve around:
*   **Inter-factional Rivalry**: Frequent armed clashes erupt between Fatah and other groups, often rooted in assassinations, feuds, or the refusal to extradite individuals.
*   **Refugee Camp Setting**: Conflicts are predominantly contained within the Palestinian refugee camps, particularly Ein el Hilweh and Mieh Mieh in Lebanon, leveraging the unique semi-autonomous status of these areas.
*   **Ceasefire Fragility**: Agreements to halt hostilities are common but frequently short-lived, with clashes often resuming shortly after, indicating deep-seated grievances and a lack of effective conflict resolution mechanisms.
*   **Spillover Risk**: Violence within the camps regularly affects surrounding Lebanese areas, with stray bullets, rockets, and shrapnel causing casualties and damage outside camp perimeters.

## 3. Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The conflict data reveals a distinct temporal shift in the nature and intensity of events within Community 10:

*   **2016-2019: Sporadic Incidents and De-escalation Efforts**
    This period is characterized by relatively isolated incidents, such as a single fatality from a disrupted weapons use (2016), and movements of forces (2017). Notably, there were efforts towards de-escalation, including ceasefire agreements between Fatah and Ansar Allah in Mieh Mieh (2018) and a significant weapons transfer agreement involving Fatah, Ansar Allah, and Hamas from Mieh Mieh to Ain el Hilweh (2019). Events were less frequent and generally involved lower direct fatalities.

*   **2023: Dramatic Escalation and Sustained Violence**
    The year 2023 marks a sharp increase in conflict frequency, intensity, and fatalities. Multiple armed clashes occurred, primarily in Lebanon's Ein el Hilweh camp, but also in Mieh Mieh and Beddawi.
    *   **March 2023**: Clashes between Fatah and Asbat al Ansar resulted in fatalities and injuries.
    *   **July-August 2023**: A major surge in violence in Ein el Hilweh, triggered by an assassination attempt, led to prolonged multi-day clashes between Fatah and Al Shabab al Muslim, Bilal Badr militia, and Jund al Sham. These events resulted in multiple fatalities (over 10 in some incidents) and numerous injuries, with ceasefires repeatedly failing.
    *   **September 2023**: A renewed, extended period of armed clashes in Ein el Hilweh, lasting several days, again between Fatah and the Joint Palestinian Security Forces against Jund al Sham and Al Shabab al Muslim. These clashes were explicitly linked to the "unwillingness of the militias to hand over the killers of a Fatah commander." This period also saw significant fatalities (e.g., 4 on September 9th, 4 on September 10th, 3 on September 11th, 7 on September 13th) and widespread injuries, with ceasefire agreements repeatedly proving ineffective.
    *   **October 2023**: Further armed clashes erupted in Ein el Hilweh involving Fatah and an unidentified armed group, resulting in another fatality.

## 4. Severity Assessment

Based on the provided metrics and conflict data:

*   **Historical Severity (0-100): 49.6**
    This metric reflects the overall conflict intensity across the entire analyzed period. The value of 49.6 indicates a moderate historical severity, consistent with the earlier period (2016-2019) which saw fewer high-fatality events and instances of agreements, alongside some isolated violent occurrences.

*   **Current Severity (0-100): 57.6**
    The current severity of 57.6 demonstrates a notable increase from the historical average. This higher value is directly attributable to the intense and prolonged armed clashes experienced throughout 2023, particularly in Ein el Hilweh. The high frequency of engagements, increased fatality counts in individual events, and the involvement of multiple factions mark a significantly more volatile and dangerous environment compared to previous years.

*   **Escalation Risk (0-100): 100.0**
    The maximum escalation risk score of 100.0 is entirely justified by the observed patterns in 2023. The data explicitly shows:
    *   **Repeated Failure of Ceasefires**: Numerous agreements were reached and subsequently broken, often within hours, leading to sustained multi-day conflicts.
    *   **Persistent Grievances**: Conflicts were frequently triggered by unaddressed issues, such as the unwillingness of groups to hand over individuals implicated in assassinations, suggesting deep-seated and unresolved tensions.
    *   **High Intensity and Lethality**: The 2023 clashes involved heavy weaponry (machine guns, RPGs, mortars, rockets, grenades) and resulted in significant fatalities and injuries among both combatants and civilians.
    *   **Geographic Concentration**: The intense focus of recent clashes in Ein el Hilweh indicates a localized but highly active flashpoint with high potential for renewed violence.
    These factors collectively underscore an exceptionally high likelihood of continued and potentially escalating conflict in the immediate future.

## 5. Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The ongoing conflict within Community 10 carries significant strategic and humanitarian implications:

*   **Strategic Impact**:
    *   **Destabilization of Palestinian Camps**: The persistent inter-factional clashes severely undermine internal stability and governance within Palestinian refugee camps in Lebanon, which operate largely outside direct Lebanese state control.
    *   **Risk of Regional Spillover**: The frequent spillover of violence into surrounding Lebanese areas (e.g., Sidon, Ghazieh) through stray bullets and rockets poses a direct threat to Lebanese civilian lives and property. This challenges Lebanese sovereignty and could draw Lebanese security forces into the internal Palestinian conflicts, further destabilizing the fragile political and security landscape of Lebanon.
    *   **Undermining Peace Efforts**: The repeated failure of ceasefire agreements highlights a fundamental breakdown in dialogue and trust among factions, impeding any prospects for sustainable peace or political resolution within the camps.

*   **Humanitarian Consequences**:
    *   **Civilian Casualties**: The documented fatalities and numerous injuries include civilians, women, and children, demonstrating the direct human cost of the conflict.
    *   **Displacement and Disruption**: Hundreds of camp residents have been forced to flee or seek refuge, disrupting daily life and exacerbating humanitarian needs within already vulnerable populations.
    *   **Damage to Infrastructure**: UNRWA schools, mosques, and residential buildings have been damaged by fighting, further deteriorating living conditions and impacting essential services like education.
    *   **Psychological Trauma**: The continuous exposure to violence, particularly for children, contributes to significant psychological distress and trauma within the camp populations.
