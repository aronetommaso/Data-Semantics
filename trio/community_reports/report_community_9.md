# Community Report: Yemen Tribal Militia Conflicts (Community 9)

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes the conflict dynamics within "community_9," primarily composed of tribal militias operating in various regions of Yemen. The period under review (2017-2023) reveals a significant temporal shift from sporadic, politically-aligned incidents to a concentrated and escalating pattern of inter-tribal armed conflict. Key drivers include deeply entrenched land disputes, competition over resources, and cycles of tribal revenge, often exacerbated by the involvement of external actors such as Houthi forces and the erstwhile Hadi government. While historical severity has been moderate, current indicators point to a notable increase in conflict intensity, culminating in an extremely high escalation risk for the foreseeable future.

## Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict landscape within Community 9 is characterized by the active participation of several prominent Yemeni tribal militias. Key actors include:

*   **Al Yaslam Tribal Militia**: Frequently involved in armed clashes, particularly in Shabwah province (Yashbum, Al Said), often over tribal revenge or unspecified disputes. Their conflicts include groups like Al Shaqra, Al Salim, and Al Bazaal.
*   **Dhu Muhammad Tribal Militia**: A recurring target in Al Jawf province (Wadi Madhab, Al Yatimah), primarily entangled in land disputes. They have clashed with Sufyan tribesmen and Houthi-backed Al Salim tribesmen.
*   **Salim Bin Daha Tribal Militia (Al Salim)**: Notably active in Al Jawf (Wadi Madhab) where they are described as Houthi-backed and involved in persistent land disputes against the Dhu Muhammad tribe. They are also involved in tribal revenge clashes with Al Yaslam and Al Awad bin Ali tribesmen in Shabwah.
*   **Sufyan Tribal Militia**: Engaged in armed clashes over land disputes in Al Jawf (Wadi Madhab), with reports indicating their members may also serve as Houthi soldiers.
*   **Al Awad Tribal Militia**: While appearing in an earlier political context, their involvement in pledging support to pro-Hadi forces highlights the broader political affiliations of some tribal groups.
*   Other significant groups involved as adversaries include Al Shaqra, Dhu Husayn, Al Bazaal, Al Awad bin Ali, Al Salim Laqran, and Al Ash Shaddad tribesmen.

The primary conflict dynamics observed are:
*   **Land Disputes**: A critical and recurrent trigger for violence, particularly evident in the protracted clashes involving the Dhu Muhammad, Sufyan, and Al Salim tribes in Al Jawf. These disputes sometimes involve attempts by Houthi forces to seize tribal lands.
*   **Tribal Revenge**: A deep-seated cause of inter-tribal clashes, especially noted in the Yashbum and Hada regions of Shabwah, involving groups like Al Yaslam, Al Salim, Al Awad bin Ali, and Al Ash Shaddad.
*   **Resource Control**: Instances of conflict arising from control over vital resources, such as the Marbun water dam, indicating competition for essential assets.
*   **Political Alignment**: Earlier events demonstrate tribal groups aligning with broader national conflict factions (e.g., pro-Hadi forces), while more recent incidents highlight external actors (e.g., Houthis) leveraging tribal disputes.

## Temporal Evolution (2017-2023)

The conflict data reveals a stark increase in activity and a shift in the nature of engagement within Community 9 over the observed period:

*   **2017**: A single event is recorded, involving the Al Awadi tribe pledging support to pro-Hadi forces. This incident, while significant for its political alignment, resulted in no direct hostilities or fatalities and represents a period of relatively low direct tribal militia conflict within the provided sample.

*   **2023**: This year marks a dramatic intensification of conflict.
    *   **Early 2023 (February-May)**: The year began with the first recorded fatality in an inter-tribal clash (Al Yaslam vs. Al Shaqra). This was followed by more severe clashes over land disputes (Sufyan vs. Dhu Muhammad), resulting in multiple fatalities, and the deployment of remote explosive devices.
    *   **Mid-2023 (June-July)**: A cluster of armed clashes occurred, primarily in Shabwah and Al Jawf. Repeated engagements involving Al Yaslam in Shabwah (Yashbum, Al Said) were noted, including disputes over water infrastructure. Concurrently, Al Jawf saw a prolonged series of clashes (three consecutive days) between Houthi-backed Al Salim and Dhu Muhammad tribesmen over land, highlighting persistent and externally influenced disputes.
    *   **Late 2023 (September-December)**: The latter part of the year continued the trend of frequent armed clashes. These were predominantly characterized by "tribal revenge" as the stated cause, occurring repeatedly in Yashbum and Hada (Shabwah). The sustained nature of these retaliatory conflicts, often involving the same sets of actors (e.g., Al Yaslam vs. Al Salim), indicates deeply entrenched grievances and a cycle of violence.

Overall, the period shows a clear evolution from limited political maneuvering in 2017 to a complex, multi-faceted, and highly active pattern of inter-tribal armed conflict throughout 2023, characterized by direct hostilities, fatalities, and localized but persistent disputes.

*   **Obstruction of Development**: Land disputes, control over resources, and general insecurity hinder economic activity, infrastructure development (e.g., well-drilling machine destroyed), and recovery efforts in already impoverished areas.

*   **Limited Access to Essential Services**: Ongoing conflict often impedes the delivery of humanitarian aid, healthcare, and education, making vulnerable populations even more susceptible to crises.
*   **Psychological Trauma**: Exposure to sustained violence, loss of property, and the constant threat of conflict can inflict severe psychological trauma on affected communities, particularly children.

The confluence of increasing severity and extreme escalation risk suggests that the humanitarian crisis in these regions is likely to deepen, requiring urgent attention and robust conflict resolution mechanisms.
