## Community Report: Conflict Dynamics in Community 5 (Middle East)

### 1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes conflict events within Community 5, an actor cluster predominantly centered on Iran and its immediate regional interactions in the Middle East, spanning the period from March 2015 to December 2023. The conflict environment within this community is characterized by persistently high levels of severity, with a notable increase in recent periods, and an extremely high risk of further escalation. Key dynamics include internal security challenges, particularly related to protests and border control; the enduring humanitarian impact of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) from past conflicts; and external military engagements involving Iranian state actors, regional non-state groups, and international forces. The cumulative effect of these factors points to a deeply unstable and volatile security landscape.

### 2. Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict events within Community 5 involve a complex interplay of state and non-state actors operating across multiple theaters.

*   **Iranian State Actors (Military Forces of Iran, Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), Police Forces of Iran, Islamic Republic of Iran Border Guard Command, Government of Iran):** These actors are central to the community's conflict dynamics. Internally, they are responsible for law enforcement, suppression of dissent (e.g., numerous arrests, crackdowns on protests, actions against "Kolbars" and smugglers), and counter-terrorism operations. Externally, the IRGC and regular military forces project influence and establish bases in Syria, move forces and provide support in regional conflicts (e.g., Yemen, Iraq), and engage in maritime security operations (e.g., tanker seizures). A tragic incident involving the IRGC downing of a Ukrainian passenger jet in 2020 highlights the high-stakes operational environment.
*   **Unidentified Military Forces:** This category represents a significant and persistent source of conflict, primarily through the detonation of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW), particularly from the Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988), across large swathes of Iran, as well as in bordering Iraq and Lebanon. These incidents cause regular fatalities and injuries to civilians, mine clearance workers, and security personnel. Unidentified forces are also implicated in various air/drone strikes and IED attacks in Syria and Yemen.
*   **External State Actors (Military Forces of the United States, Global Coalition Against Daesh, Military Forces of Turkey, Military Forces of Israel, Military Forces of Russia, Military Forces of Syria):** These actors are primarily involved in Syria and Iraq, conducting security measures, establishing bases, engaging in reconnaissance and strike operations (e.g., drone interceptions, airstrikes), and in some cases, directly clashing with Iranian-backed forces. Their presence contributes to regional geopolitical tension and proxy conflicts.
*   **Non-State Armed Groups (Army of Justice, Ansar al Furqan, Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL)/Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP), Kataib Hezbollah (Iraq), Houthi forces, PJAK, Unidentified Armed Group Iran, Unidentified Tribal Militia Yemen, Opposition Rebels Syria, Deir-ez-Zor Revolutionaries Gathering, Qashqai Tribal Militia Iran):** These groups represent a diverse array of threats. In Iran, groups like the Army of Justice, Ansar al Furqan, and ISKP carry out high-fatality attacks on police and civilian targets, particularly in the southeastern provinces. In Syria, various anti-Iran groups conduct attacks, while Iranian-backed militias like Kataib Hezbollah are active. Houthi forces in Yemen engage in drone interceptions. These groups contribute significantly to armed clashes and remote attacks.
*   **Civilian Actors (Rioters, Protesters, Smugglers/Kolbars, Activists):** Civilian populations are frequently victims of conflict, but also active participants in protests (e.g., Mahsa Amini movement, fuel price protests) against state policies. "Kolbars" (Kurdish cross-border porters) and other smugglers, particularly in border regions, face lethal force from Iranian border guards, contributing to significant human rights concerns and internal tensions.

### 3. Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The conflict landscape within Community 5 demonstrates both continuity in certain conflict types and significant shifts in intensity and focus over the analyzed period.

*   **2015-2016: Persistent Legacy and Initial Regional Engagements:** The early period is dominated by a high frequency of ERW incidents in Iran, causing numerous civilian casualties. Iranian military forces initiate logistical support and movement in Yemen and Syria. Internal arrests by police for social gatherings also begin to be recorded.
*   **2017-2018: Escalation of External Projection and Internal Suppression:** Iranian IRGC forces establish significant military bases and move personnel in Syria (e.g., Kisweh, Al-Bukamal, Tiyas Airbase, Kirkuk in Iraq), indicating a deepening regional involvement. Simultaneously, internal security forces increase arrests related to alleged militant links and crackdowns on various social and ethnic groups. US military forces become more active in Iraq and Syria, establishing bases and patrolling.
*   **2019: Heightened Internal Unrest and Cross-Border Tensions:** This year sees a sharp rise in internal protests (e.g., fuel prices), met with widespread arrests and lethal force. ERW incidents continue their steady toll. Cross-border tensions involving Iranian border guards and smugglers/Kolbars result in injuries. Iranian and US forces conduct various movements in Iraq and Syria, signaling continued strategic competition.
*   **2020: Catastrophic Air Incident and Continued ERW:** The tragic shooting down of Ukraine International Airlines Flight 752 by the IRGC, killing 176, marks a peak in fatalities from direct state action. ERW incidents persist with lethal consequences. The Iranian government implements widespread security measures, partly linked to COVID-19 and anniversaries of protests, often involving extensive force deployment.
*   **2021: Sustained Border Violence and Regional Maneuvers:** Fatalities and injuries from ERW continue unabated. Border guards maintain a high level of force against Kolbars and fuel smugglers, particularly affecting Baloch and Kurdish communities. Iranian IRGC forces continue military movements and deployments in Syria, while US/Global Coalition forces engage in drone activities.
*   **2022: Major Internal Crisis and Intensified Lethality:** This year marks a significant increase in internal conflict, particularly in the wake of the Mahsa Amini protests. The Zahedan incident alone results in over 100 fatalities, highlighting the scale of state repression and civilian resistance. Non-state armed groups (e.g., Army of Justice, ISKP) conduct more lethal attacks. ERW continues to cause deaths and injuries.
*   **2023 (Partial): Peak of Lethality and Multi-Front Confrontation:** The trend of increased lethality continues. High-fatality incidents occur from clashes between Iranian border guards and various armed groups (e.g., Army of Justice, Taliban) on its eastern borders. ERW maintains its deadly impact. In Syria, clashes involving Iranian-backed militias, Syrian forces, and unidentified armed groups result in significant fatalities. The attack on the Shah Cheragh Shrine by ISKP demonstrates the persistent threat of transnational terrorism.

### 4. Severity Assessment

Based on the provided pre-calculated metrics, the severity of conflict within Community 5 is exceptionally high across all indicators:

*   **Historical Severity (0-100): 85.2**
    This metric indicates a long-standing and consistently high level of conflict intensity throughout the 2015-2023 period. The frequent occurrence of ERW incidents, which regularly cause fatalities and injuries to civilians and security personnel, forms a persistent baseline of violence. Alongside this, the continuous pattern of internal arrests, suppression of protests, and often lethal engagements by Iranian border forces against smugglers and Kolbars, even if individual incidents are not always high-fatality, contribute to a high cumulative toll and a pervasive environment of insecurity. The intermittent but significant military actions and proxy conflicts involving Iranian state actors in Syria, Yemen, and Iraq further compound this historical baseline, preventing any sustained reduction in overall severity.

*   **Current Severity (0-100): 95.6**
    The current severity score reflects a marked escalation in the recent conflict environment. This increase is primarily driven by several factors:
    1.  **Increased Lethality of Internal Conflicts:** Events such as the Zahedan incident (September 2022, 103 fatalities), clashes with the Army of Justice and Ansar al Furqan in Sistan and Baluchestan (2023, high fatalities), and the Iranian state's violent suppression of the Mahsa Amini protests have resulted in a significantly higher number of fatalities and injuries.
    2.  **Persistent and Deadly ERW:** While a constant, the sheer volume and continued lethality of ERW incidents, particularly in border regions, remains a critical contributor to ongoing severity, claiming lives regularly.
    3.  **Active External Engagement:** Iranian military forces continue their extensive involvement in Syria, facing attacks from various actors, including suspected Israeli airstrikes and local armed groups, resulting in casualties.
    4.  **Targeted Attacks by Non-State Actors:** The occurrence of sophisticated and high-casualty attacks by groups like ISKP (Shah Cheragh Shrine, August 2023, 4 fatalities) and the Army of Justice, targeting security forces and civilians, indicates a volatile and dangerous operational environment.

*   **Escalation Risk (0-100): 97.6**
    The extremely high escalation risk score is justified by a confluence of deeply entrenched and volatile factors:
    1.  **Entrenched Internal Discontent:** Ongoing widespread protests, particularly in response to social, economic, and political grievances (e.g., Mahsa Amini movement), and the government's consistent use of force to suppress them, create a perpetual cycle of unrest with high potential for renewed and intensified violence. Ethnic tensions, particularly in Kurdish and Baloch regions, exacerbate this risk.
    2.  **Multi-Front Regional Proxy Conflicts:** Iranian strategic depth operations and support for proxies in Syria, Yemen, and Iraq place its forces in direct or indirect confrontation with a diverse array of adversaries, including the US, Israel, and various non-state armed groups. This complex web of engagements presents numerous flashpoints for miscalculation and wider regional conflict.
    3.  **Cross-Border Instability:** The highly militarized and often violent interactions along Iran's borders, especially with Pakistan and Afghanistan (clashes with Army of Justice, Taliban), and the persistent lethal force against smugglers and Kolbars, mean that local incidents can quickly escalate into larger confrontations or fuel broader ethnic grievances.
    4.  **Proliferation of Armed Groups:** The presence and active operations of various armed groups, both internal (e.g., Army of Justice, Ansar al Furqan) and regional (e.g., ISKP, Houthi), capable of inflicting significant casualties, ensure a continuous threat and contribute to a highly unstable security environment.
    5.  **Humanitarian Crisis from ERW:** The enduring impact of ERW, while not directly an act of aggression, contributes to a state of perpetual humanitarian crisis and resentment, which can feed into broader instability.

### 5. Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

**Strategic Impact:**
The conflict dynamics within Community 5 have profound strategic implications for both Iran and the wider Middle East. Internally, the Iranian government faces significant challenges to its legitimacy and stability due to widespread protests, ethnic grievances, and a heavy-handed security response. This internal pressure is compounded by the persistent threat from non-state armed groups. Regionally, Iran's assertive foreign policy, particularly its military presence and support for proxies in Syria, Yemen, and Iraq, has directly fueled protracted conflicts and contributed to a highly militarized environment. This has, in turn, drawn in major international powers (e.g., the US, Global Coalition) and regional rivals (e.g., Israel, Turkey), creating a complex and volatile geopolitical chessboard where localized incidents can rapidly escalate into broader confrontations, impacting regional security architecture and international trade routes.

**Humanitarian Consequences:**
The humanitarian toll within Community 5 is severe and multifaceted. The most pervasive and insidious threat comes from the **Explosive Remnants of War (ERW)**, which consistently claims civilian lives and limbs, disrupts livelihoods, and hinders development, particularly in border provinces of Iran and parts of Iraq and Lebanon. State-led **repression of protests and border control operations** lead to widespread arrests, injuries, and fatalities among civilians, including Kolbars and fuel smugglers, often affecting vulnerable ethnic minority communities. The use of force in these contexts raises significant human rights concerns. In external theaters, particularly Syria and Yemen, Iranian-backed forces' involvement contributes to **displacement, civilian casualties from airstrikes and ground clashes**, and the disruption of essential services. The overall environment of high severity and escalating risk means that civilian populations endure chronic insecurity, economic hardship, and psychological trauma, with limited avenues for redress or protection.
