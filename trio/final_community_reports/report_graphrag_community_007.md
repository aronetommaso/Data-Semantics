# Community Report: Iraq and Syria (ISIL-focused Conflict Zone)

## Executive Summary

This report provides a structured analysis of conflict events within the defined actor cluster in Iraq and Syria, spanning from 2016 to late 2023. The data illustrates a complex and highly lethal conflict primarily centered around the activities of the Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) and multi-actor efforts to counter it. Key characteristics include periods of intense conventional warfare with high fatality counts, a subsequent transition to asymmetric insurgent tactics by ISIL, and a persistent humanitarian toll. The calculated metrics indicate an extremely high and sustained level of severity, coupled with a significant risk of escalation.

## Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict landscape in this community is dominated by several key actors and their evolving dynamics:

1.  **Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL)**: Functioning as the primary non-state actor and instigator of violence, ISIL has demonstrated capabilities ranging from large-scale military offensives and territorial control (particularly in 2016-2017) to a resilient insurgency. Their tactics include mass executions of civilians, suicide bombings, sophisticated IED networks, and targeted attacks on security forces and vulnerable populations (e.g., truffle gatherers).

2.  **Military Forces of Iraq (across various periods: 2014-2026)**: Comprising the Iraqi Army, Federal Police, and Counter-Terrorism Service (CTS), these forces have been central to ground operations, territorial reclamation, and ongoing counter-insurgency efforts. They frequently engage ISIL in armed clashes, airstrikes, and operations to destroy hideouts and defuse explosives.

3.  **Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF)**: A state-sponsored umbrella organization of predominantly Shi'a militias, the PMF have played a significant role alongside the Iraqi military in combating ISIL, particularly in ground offensives and securing recaptured territories.

4.  **Global Coalition Against Daesh (Anti-ISIL Coalition)**: Led primarily by the United States, this international coalition provides crucial air support (airstrikes), intelligence, and training to Iraqi and Syrian partners. Coalition airstrikes have frequently inflicted heavy casualties on ISIL and destroyed their assets.

5.  **Syrian Democratic Forces (QSD)**: A multi-ethnic, Kurdish-led alliance primarily active in northeast Syria, QSD has been a key partner of the Global Coalition in ground operations against ISIL, culminating in the territorial defeat of ISIL in Syria.

6.  **Military Forces of Syria (2000-2024)**: The Syrian regime forces and their allied militias have also been engaged in combat with ISIL, particularly in central and eastern Syria, often vying for control over desert areas.

7.  **Peshmerga Forces**: The military forces of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, Peshmerga have actively participated in operations against ISIL in northern Iraq, often in coordination with Iraqi federal forces and the Global Coalition.

The dynamics have shifted over time, moving from large-scale territorial warfare (2016-2017) to a more fragmented and asymmetric conflict (2018-2023), characterized by ISIL's persistent insurgent attacks and extensive counter-insurgency operations by state and allied forces.

## Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The conflict within this cluster demonstrates a distinct temporal evolution:

*   **2016-2017: Peak of Conventional Warfare and ISIL Atrocities**: This period is marked by large-scale, high-intensity military campaigns to dislodge ISIL from its strongholds in Iraq (e.g., Mosul, Fallujah) and significant operations in Syria. Fatality counts are exceptionally high, encompassing both militants and civilians. ISIL committed numerous mass executions and large-scale terror attacks, such as the devastating Baghdad suicide bombings in July 2016 (293 and 292 fatalities). Counter-ISIL forces, backed by extensive Coalition airstrikes (e.g., Ramadi, 250 ISIL fatalities; Al Muhammadi, 78 ISIL fatalities), successfully reclaimed significant territory. Civilian casualties were also high from both ISIL actions and military operations (e.g., Mosul airstrikes, 230 civilians killed).

*   **2018-2019: Post-Territorial Defeat and Insurgency Emergence**: Following major territorial losses (e.g., fall of Baghuz in 2019), ISIL shifted towards an entrenched insurgency. Direct large-scale clashes became less frequent, replaced by more asymmetric tactics. A notable increase in "Disrupted weapons use" events (IED planting failures, defusals, seizures) indicates ISIL's reliance on improvised explosive devices. Mass graves continued to be discovered (e.g., Hawija, Fallujah), revealing the extent of ISIL's past atrocities. Civilian displacement due to lingering ISIL activity or explosive remnants of war (ERW) also became more prominent.

*   **2020-2023: Persistent Low-Level Insurgency and ERW Legacy**: The most recent years show a continued pattern of ISIL's low-intensity insurgent activities, primarily in remote or contested areas of Iraq (e.g., Hamrin Mountains) and the Syrian desert. Attacks often involve IEDs, ambushes, and targeting of vulnerable groups. Incidents of ISIL militants destroying surveillance cameras or hideouts indicate a struggle for control in peripheral zones. A concerning trend in 2023 is ISIL's repeated targeting of truffle gatherers in Syria, resulting in significant civilian fatalities (e.g., Wadi Dabiyat, 68 fatalities; Oqeirbat, 36 fatalities). Meanwhile, the long-term humanitarian impact of ERW from previous conflicts (Iran-Iraq war, Saddam era) continues to cause civilian casualties across Iraq. Counter-insurgency efforts by Iraqi and Syrian forces, often with Coalition support, remain focused on search-and-destroy missions, intelligence-led arrests, and defusal operations to disrupt ISIL networks.

The widespread territorial control by ISIL, coupled with its brutal tactics and the extensive military campaigns required to dislodge it, created a catastrophic humanitarian and security environment, fully justifying a maximum historical severity score.

The data for 2020-2023 demonstrates ongoing, albeit different, forms of high-impact violence.     3.  **Fragmented Security Landscape**: The involvement of numerous state and non-state actors (Iraqi forces, PMF, Peshmerga, SDF, Syrian regime forces, various militias, and the Global Coalition) creates a complex and sometimes uncoordinated security environment, which ISIL can exploit.
    4.  **Enduring Root Causes**: While not explicitly detailed in the event descriptions, the historical context of the conflict suggests underlying political, social, and economic grievances that could fuel a resurgence of extremist groups or further destabilize the region.
    5.  **Ongoing Fatalities**: The consistent reporting of fatalities, including significant civilian deaths and deaths among security forces, underscores the active nature of the conflict and the potential for a return to higher intensity, particularly if regional dynamics shift or counter-terrorism pressure diminishes.

## Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The conflict in this region has generated profound and enduring strategic and humanitarian consequences:

### Strategic Impact:
*   **Persistent Destabilization**: The prolonged conflict has severely destabilized both Iraq and Syria, eroding state authority in large areas and contributing to regional insecurity. Even after territorial defeat, ISIL's continued insurgent activities prevent full stabilization and recovery.
*   **Diversion of Resources**: Significant national and international military, financial, and political resources continue to be diverted to counter-terrorism efforts, hindering economic development and post-conflict reconstruction.
*   **International Involvement**: The sustained presence and operations of the Global Coalition underscore the international recognition of ISIL as a transnational threat, highlighting the global strategic implications of the conflict.
*   **Complex Governance Challenges**: The involvement of diverse state and non-state armed groups (PMF, Peshmerga, SDF, various pro-regime militias) has complicated governance structures and security arrangements, creating potential for internal tensions and future conflicts.

### Humanitarian Consequences:
*   **Massive Civilian Casualties**: The dataset tragically highlights the immense loss of civilian life through direct attacks, executions, suicide bombings, and collateral damage from military operations. Mass graves serve as grim reminders of the atrocities committed, particularly by ISIL.
*   **Widespread Displacement**: Millions have been displaced from their homes, leading to protracted humanitarian crises, internal displacement, and refugee flows, straining resources and creating significant social and economic challenges.
*   **Explosive Remnants of War (ERW)**: A devastating legacy of the conflict is the widespread contamination by ERW and landmines. These unexploded devices continue to pose an existential threat to civilians, years after active combat has ceased, often leading to fatalities and injuries, particularly among children and agricultural workers.
*   **Infrastructure Destruction**: Years of intense fighting have resulted in extensive damage to infrastructure, including homes, public services, and economic assets, impeding recovery and exacerbating humanitarian needs.
*   **Trauma and Psychological Impact**: The sheer scale of violence, loss, and displacement has left deep psychological scars on the affected populations, necessitating long-term mental health and psychosocial support.
*   **Food Insecurity and Livelihood Disruption**: The targeting of agricultural workers (e.g., truffle pickers) and the general insecurity in rural areas directly impact livelihoods and food security, pushing vulnerable communities deeper into hardship.
