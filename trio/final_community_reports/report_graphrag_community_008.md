## Community Report: Iran and Wider Middle East (Actor Cluster)

### 1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes conflict events within the "Iran and Wider Middle East" cluster from 2015 to late 2023, focusing on a complex interplay of domestic challenges, regional projection, and external interventions. Key dynamics include the pervasive threat of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) within Iran and neighboring Iraq, extensive domestic security operations by Iranian police and military forces involving widespread arrests and suppression of dissent, and Iran's strategic military and proxy presence in Syria and Yemen. Counter-actions by US and Global Coalition forces, primarily through troop movements, base establishments, and drone interceptions, underscore regional tensions. The period also features significant instances of violence from non-state armed groups targeting Iranian forces and a tragic civilian aviation incident. The overall severity and risk of escalation remain exceptionally high, reflecting a deeply volatile environment.

### 2. Primary Actors & Dynamics

The conflict events in this cluster are shaped by several dominant actors and their interactions:

*   **Iranian State Actors (Military Forces of Iran, Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), Police Forces of Iran, Islamic Republic of Iran Border Guard Command, Government of Iran):** These actors are central to the cluster. Domestically, they are heavily involved in law enforcement (mass arrests at parties, during protests, against smugglers), internal security (weapons seizures, anti-terrorism plots), and border control (shooting of "Kolbars" and fuel smugglers). Regionally, the IRGC and Iranian military are engaged in force projection, establishing bases and moving equipment and personnel in Syria (Kisweh, Al-Bukamal, Tiyas Airbase, Al Mayadin) and providing support in Yemen. They are also involved in naval incidents and directly responsible for the tragic downing of a civilian airliner in 2020.
*   **Unidentified Military Forces:** This category is primarily responsible for the overwhelming number of "Explosive Remnants of War" (ERW) events, causing persistent civilian and military casualties, particularly along the Iran-Iraq border. These remnants are legacies of the Iran-Iraq War (1980s). Unidentified forces are also implicated in various drone activities, including strikes and crashes, across Syria, Yemen, and Lebanon.
*   **United States Military Forces (and Global Coalition Against Daesh):** The US maintains a significant regional presence, characterized by troop movements (Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Yemen), establishment of military bases (Syria, Iraq, Yemen), and security measures like joint patrols and air defense systems. They are frequently involved in intercepting unidentified drones or vessels, often in contexts related to countering Iranian influence or other regional threats.
*   **Non-State Armed Groups (e.g., Army of Justice, Ansar al Furqan, PJAK, ISIL, Deir-ez-Zor Revolutionaries Gathering, Kataib Hezbollah Iraq):** These groups contribute to the cluster's violence through attacks on Iranian security forces (especially in Sistan and Baluchestan), clashes, and recruitment drives. ISIL and other groups are responsible for attacks and shelling against Iranian-affiliated forces in Syria and domestic terrorist incidents in Iran (Shiraz shrine attack).
*   **Local Populations/Civilians:** Civilians, including border porters (Kolbars) and fuel smugglers, are frequently caught in the crossfire of border control operations and are disproportionately affected by ERW. Domestic protests in Iran lead to widespread arrests and, at times, violent suppression, causing civilian fatalities.

### 3. Temporal Evolution (2015-2023)

The cluster's dynamics exhibit a clear evolution:

*   **2015-2016: Persistent ERW and Domestic Control.** The early period is dominated by a high frequency of ERW incidents in Iran, causing numerous injuries and some fatalities. Iranian police forces conduct numerous arrests, primarily targeting social gatherings and perceived moral infractions. Minor Iranian military movements and agreements in Yemen and Syria are noted.
*   **2017-2018: Escalating Regional Projection and Counter-Actions.** While ERW continues to be a major issue in Iran, this period sees a marked increase in Iran's military and IRGC presence in Syria and Iraq, including the establishment of key bases. Concurrently, US military forces intensify their movements, security measures, and base establishments in the region, often in direct or indirect response to Iranian activities. Domestic arrests in Iran broaden to include suspected militants and ethnic activists.
*   **2019-2021: Major Fatalities, Domestic Unrest, and Cyber Attacks.** ERW remains a consistent threat. The period is tragically marked by the downing of Ukraine International Airlines Flight 752 by the IRGC in January 2020, killing 176. Iranian domestic politics see large-scale fuel price protests (late 2019) leading to thousands of arrests and significant security force deployments. Cross-border incidents involving "Kolbars" and fuel smugglers facing fire from Iranian border guards continue, often with fatalities. Regional drone activities, including interceptions by US/Global Coalition forces, highlight ongoing air domain tensions. A significant cyberattack impacts Iran's gas subsidy system in 2021.
*   **2022-2023: Heightened Internal Violence and Regional Clashes.** The trend of ERW persists. Domestically, Iran experiences major unrest following Mahsa Amini's death (late 2022), leading to mass protests, widespread arrests, and violent crackdowns, including the Zahedan incident with over 100 fatalities. Non-state armed groups like the Army of Justice and Ansar al Furqan become more active, engaging in deadly armed clashes with Iranian police and IRGC, particularly in Sistan and Baluchestan. Regionally, there are continued drone strikes, often fatal, targeting pro-Iran militias in Syria by unidentified forces (suspected US/Israel), and armed clashes between Iranian-backed forces and other groups. Deadly border clashes with Afghanistan (Taliban) also occur.

### 4. Severity Assessment

The conflict events in this cluster are characterized by exceptionally high and increasing severity, coupled with a very high risk of further escalation, as evidenced by the provided metrics:

*   **Historical Severity (0-100): 85.2**
    This high score reflects a prolonged period of multi-faceted conflict. Key factors contributing to this severity include:
    *   **Persistent Fatalities from ERW:** The continuous toll on civilians, mine clearers, and military personnel from landmines and unexploded ordnance dating back to the Iran-Iraq War demonstrates an enduring humanitarian crisis.
    *   **Significant Domestic Repression:** Widespread arrests, crackdowns on protests (e.g., fuel protests 2019, Mahsa Amini protests 2022), and fatal incidents involving border guards and smugglers contribute to a high level of internal state violence.
    *   **Major Single Incidents:** The accidental downing of a civilian airliner in January 2020 (176 fatalities) and the Zahedan violence in September 2022 (103+ fatalities) are catastrophic events that significantly elevate historical severity.
    *   **Regional Military Engagements:** Iran's sustained military build-up and operations in Syria and Yemen, often involving proxies and leading to clashes, underscore the strategic intensity.

*   **Current Severity (0-100): 95.6**
    The increase from historical to current severity indicates a worsening situation. The latter part of the dataset, particularly late 2022 and 2023, shows a pronounced escalation:
    *   **Intensified Domestic Armed Clashes:** Recent events highlight increased and deadly armed clashes within Iran, particularly in Sistan and Baluchestan, involving groups like the Army of Justice and Ansar al Furqan targeting police and IRGC forces (e.g., 5 officers killed in Saravan in May 2023, 13 fatalities in Rasak in December 2023). This points to a more volatile internal security landscape.
    *   **Continued and Deadly ERW:** Fatalities from ERW persist, including those affecting border guards and civilians, demonstrating the ongoing, deadly legacy.
    *   **Persistent Regional Drone Strikes and Violence:** Fatal drone strikes against pro-Iranian militias in Syria by unidentified forces continue, indicating an active and deadly proxy conflict.
    *   **Border Skirmishes:** Deadly clashes at the Iran-Afghanistan border in May and July 2023 add another dimension of external tension.

*   **Escalation Risk (0-100): 97.6**
    This exceptionally high score signifies a critical level of risk for further intensification of conflict. The factors underpinning this assessment include:
    *   **Entrenched Internal Discontent:** The ongoing domestic protests, compounded by severe state responses and the emergence of more active armed resistance, create a fertile ground for further cycles of violence and repression.
    *   **Deep Regional Proxy Conflicts:** Iran's persistent regional projection, coupled with active counter-operations by regional and international actors (US, Israel), ensures continued friction and potential for direct confrontation. The establishment and targeting of bases in Syria and drone intercepts are clear indicators.
    *   **Legacy of Unresolved Conflicts:** The pervasive ERW problem ensures a steady stream of casualties, creating a humanitarian and security challenge that feeds into local grievances.
    *   **Unidentified Actors and Unpredictability:** The frequent involvement of "unidentified military forces" in drone strikes and ERW incidents adds layers of ambiguity and unpredictability, making de-escalation difficult and increasing the risk of miscalculation.
    *   **Recent Deadly Incidents:** The clusters of fatal clashes in late 2023 demonstrate that the capacity and willingness for lethal engagements are actively present and occurring.

### 5. Strategic Impact & Humanitarian Consequences

The dynamics within this cluster have profound strategic and humanitarian implications:

*   **Strategic Impact:** The region serves as a major arena for geopolitical competition, with Iran's efforts to project influence clashing with US and Israeli strategic interests. This leads to a complex web of proxy conflicts, military build-ups, and targeted strikes that maintain an elevated state of tension. The proliferation of military bases and movement of forces by various actors (Iran, US, Russia) highlights the militarization of the region. Internal stability in Iran, challenged by protests and armed group activities, remains a critical strategic concern both for the Iranian government and international observers.
*   **Humanitarian Consequences:** The humanitarian toll is severe and multifaceted. ERW pose a constant threat to civilian life and livelihoods, particularly for agricultural workers and border communities. Domestic security crackdowns result in mass arrests, injuries, and fatalities, impacting human rights and civil liberties. Border communities, especially "Kolbars" and fuel smugglers, face significant risks of death or injury from security forces. Major incidents like the airline crash and the Zahedan violence underscore the devastating potential for large-scale loss of life. The persistent violence and insecurity contribute to civilian suffering, displacement (though not directly highlighted as a major theme in this sample), and a pervasive climate of fear.
